## 1. What the issue asks, and what it has already decided

Goal (quoted): *"No resolution, aspect ratio or field of view is special anywhere in the engine:
every screen-dependent decision is computed from the actual viewport at runtime, the invariants …
hold as properties over a continuous range of viewports from 320×180 to 7680×2160 and 1:1 to 32:9,
and the cost at any size is measured and left to the existing per-PC quality machinery."*

Binding design decisions:

1. *"derived from the actual viewport: pixel width, pixel height and vertical FOV, through one type
   (`Viewport { width_px, height_px, fov_y }` in `cubara-render`, with `fov_x()`, `rad_per_px_y()`,
   `rad_per_px_x()`), passed in (Rule 2) … no constant such as `1080`, `"7 px at 1080p"` or
   `0.97 mrad` survives outside the gate bench's own `DEFAULT_SIZE` and the test samples.
   Thresholds are stated in pixels of the current viewport."*
2. *"vertical FOV fixed (60° today), horizontal follows the aspect (Hor+)"*. The 130° cap is struck.
2a. *"No FOV limit … the renderer draws a frame as one or more views: one eye, N view directions
   and frusta."* Shared once per frame: LOD selection, streaming, meshing. Per view: frustum
   culling, the tree cull, the far draw list. *"what is drawn per view is that view's visible set
   and nothing wider."* Tests: random view sets (1–4 views, horizontal FOV 30°–179° each, up to
   360°); *"LOD and the far terrain's error bound hold in pixels of each view."* Scope: *"makes the
   engine correct for N views and adds a headless multi-view render with a golden at a 3-view, 360°
   setup. Opening several real windows … is a follow-up."*
3. *"Tests are properties over a continuous range, not hand-picked sizes. A seeded sweep of random
   viewports, width 320–7680, height 180–2160, aspect 1:1–32:9 (at least 64 per test run; the seed
   printed on failure), asserting: … T1 … T4 … no node/far seam above tolerance at the join … the
   HUD and menus fit"*. Three goldens (16:9, 32:9, ≈20:13) *"only as visual pins"*.
4. *"`--bench gate --size` runs a sweep (at least 1280×720, 1920×1080, 2560×1664, 3840×2160,
   5120×1440) … no special-casing per resolution."*
5. UI scale: under this issue's sweep or a linked sibling; *"The lead decides the split."*
6. *"the gate's canonical row keeps `DEFAULT_SIZE` … a bench constant, read by the bench only."*

Out of scope (quoted): *"A user-facing FOV setting … upscaling … HiDPI beyond `design-ui-scale.md`
… The ring schedule's constants `N_L` (what step size is acceptable is the owner's eye and the gate;
this issue makes them resolution-free, not different)."*

Done-when that shapes the tests: *"no existing golden changes at `DEFAULT_SIZE` (the derivation
returns today's radii at 1920×1080/60° exactly)"*; a `check-architecture.sh` grep for
`\b(1080|1920)\b` with its fixture; a `check-tests-can-fail.sh` table per PR *"including a mutation
that reintroduces a fixed 1080 in the ring radius derivation"*.

Decided outside the issue: exit-gate item 0 is judged at 1920×1080. This plan changes no gate term.

## 3. Approach, and what was rejected

**One type, one scalar.** `cubara_render::Viewport` is the only place a size becomes an angle.
Everything LOD-related consumes one number, `rad_per_px()` = `fov_y / height_px` computed in
`f64`. `cubara-world` cannot depend on `cubara-render` (Rules 3/4), so world functions take
`pixel_angle: f64`.

**What that scalar is** (the doc comment of `rad_per_px` and the PHASE3 section, verbatim):
> *The LOD scalar is the mean vertical pixel angle, `fov_y / height_px`. A pixel on the view axis
> subtends `2·tan(fov_y/2)/height_px`: 10% more at 60°, so on the axis the bound is about 10%
> conservative. Toward the frame's edges a pixel subtends less angle (×cos²θ horizontally). The
> bound is angular on purpose: a bound in edge pixels grows without limit as a view approaches
> 180°, and decision 2a has no FOV limit.*

"Pixels of a view" in every test of this plan means: angle ÷ `rad_per_px()`. `rad_per_px_x()`
(`fov_x()/width_px`) exists because decision 1 names it, and is used for reporting only.

**Rings: `R_L = ceil(2^(L+1) / (N_L · p) / 16)` chunks**, `N_L` = pixels per cell just outside ring
L's edge = `[12.90, 14.33, 16.12, 16.12]`. These are **derived**, back-solved from today's radii
(exact values 12.8916 / 14.3239 / 16.1144) and rounded up in the third digit so `ceil` lands on
today's integers: `[10,18,32,64]` at 1080 rows, `[16,28,50,99]` at 1664, `[20,36,64,128]` at 2160.
`ceil`, not `round`, so an edge is never nearer than its constant allows (`round` gives a 7.16 px
step at 360 rows, over T1's 7).

**A floor on ring width.** Plain scaling breaks the octree's level balance below ≈540 rows (Python
port), which `crate::mesh`'s covered-border-face rule needs. Each ring is at least
`MIN_RING_NODES = 2` nodes of its own level wide: floor schedule `[2, 6, 14, 30]`. The floor only
moves edges outward, so every pixel bound still holds. (2 follows from the balance rule with
squash 2; the "retry with 3" in PR 3 is chosen, not derived.)

**Capacity follows demand, not a model.** The voxel arena and the far slots grow when an insert
does not fit: bigger buffers, the used prefix copied on the GPU, allocators extended. Offsets and
node indices do not change, so nothing re-streams and no frame loses geometry. This lands
**before** anything can make demand larger (PR 1b), so no later PR can ship an overflow.

**N views = N calls of one per-view function over one resident set.** The function is extracted
first (it exists three times today), then looped. The shared LOD scalar of a frame is the smallest
`rad_per_px()` among its views.

Rejected:
- *LOD from edge pixels, or `min(rad_per_px_x, rad_per_px_y)`* — unbounded toward 180°; would not
  return today's radii at the gate.
- *Keep fixed rings and scale the thresholds* — contradicts decision 1 verbatim.
- *A node cap / "above N rows do X"* — decision 4 forbids it.
- *Capacity from a footprint model + reset-and-re-stream on resize* (revision 1) — drops every
  resident node when the window grows (holes on going fullscreen), and needs a model that must
  itself be proven.
- *One wide or cylindrical projection for >180°* — a second projection and shader path.
- *wgpu multiview* — `mesh.wgsl` bakes the viewport as pipeline overrides for a measured M3 reason
  (`render.rs:1453-1462`).
- *A new RNG crate* — nothing may be installed; SplitMix64 is six lines.

### PR 1b — capacity as a value that grows on demand (no change at the gate)

Branch `feat/394-capacity`. Render crate, plus the far-insert call site in the app.

1. `arena.rs`:
   ```rust
   #[derive(Clone, Copy, Debug, PartialEq, Eq)]
   pub struct ArenaCapacity { pub vertices: u32, pub indices: u32, pub nodes: u32, pub draws: u32 }
   impl ArenaCapacity { pub const MEASURED: Self = Self { vertices: 4_000_000, indices: 6_000_000, nodes: 16_384, draws: 16_384 }; }
   ```
   The four constants go; `ChunkArena` holds `capacity`. `ChunkArena::new(device, multi_draw,
   anchor)` starts at `MEASURED` (so nothing changes at the gate); add
   `with_capacity(device, multi_draw, anchor, capacity)` for tests. Vertex and index buffers gain
   `BufferUsages::COPY_SRC`.
2. Growth. `insert` gains `device: &wgpu::Device` (today it takes only `queue`, `arena.rs:560-567`;
   update `ArenaSink::try_insert` in `render.rs`, `headless.rs`, `bench.rs`, tests). When any of the
   three allocations fails (`arena.rs:581-609`): free what succeeded (as today), call
   `fn grow(&mut self, device, queue, need_vertices, need_indices, need_node: bool) -> bool`, and
   retry once. `grow`:
   - new size per exhausted resource = `max(old * 3 / 2, old + need)`, capped at
     `device.limits().max_buffer_size / element_size` and, for nodes, at 65,536 (the 16-bit
     `node_index`). If the cap leaves no room for `need`, return `false` → today's warning and
     `false` from `insert` (the only remaining overflow path; a hardware limit, not a resolution).
   - vertices / indices: create the bigger buffer, `copy_buffer_to_buffer` the prefix
     `[0, high_water)` in a one-off encoder, `queue.submit` it at once (a submit flushes the
     `write_buffer`s already staged for the old buffer before the copy runs), swap the buffer,
     `SlabAllocator::extend(new_capacity)` (append or coalesce a free tail).
   - nodes: `origins` Vec resized, a new origins buffer written from that CPU copy, the bind group
     rebuilt (`origins_bind_group()` is read per frame, `arena.rs:481`); the indirect buffer
     recreated at the new `draws` (= nodes; it is rewritten every `prepare`); `NodeIndexAllocator`
     limit raised. `prepare`'s `MAX_DRAWS` check reads `self.capacity.draws`.
   - `log::info!` one line per growth with old → new sizes.
   `usage()` reports the current capacity.
3. `render/src/far.rs`: buffers `heights` and `patches` gain `COPY_SRC`; `insert(&mut self, device,
   queue, patch)`: when `self.free` is empty, grow the slots ×1.5 (cap: `max_buffer_size /
   per_patch`): new `heights`, `patches`, `draw_slots` buffers, copy the first two on the GPU the
   same way, rebuild the bind group, extend `free`, `boxes`, `placed`. `Renderer::far_insert`
   passes its device. `FAR_PATCH_CAPACITY` stays 4,096 as the **starting** size.
   `far_streaming.rs:439-448` keeps its `None` branch for the device cap, and counts it:
   `FarStreaming::undrawn() -> usize` (patches ready and wanted with no slot).
4. `crates/world/tests/radius_64_smoke.rs:84-87`: comment updated — it mirrors
   `ArenaCapacity::MEASURED`, which keeps today's values.
5. Tests:

Where this plan differs from the review:

- **R1 — "grow on exhaustion" needs a signature change the review does not mention.**
  `ChunkArena::insert(&mut self, queue, id, origin, scale, mesh, local_aabb)` (`arena.rs:560-567`)
  and `FarTerrain::insert(&mut self, queue, patch)` (`render/src/far.rs:411`) have no device, and
  the buffers are created `VERTEX | COPY_DST`, `INDEX | COPY_DST`, `STORAGE | COPY_DST`
  (`arena.rs:423,429`; `far.rs:273-278`) — no `COPY_SRC`. So PR 1b adds `device` to both inserts
  and a usage bit to four buffers; the latter is a standing change at the gate and is why PR 1b
  carries a gate pair rather than "nothing changes".
- **R2 — S1's baseline.** The review compares against ⁸⁸'s rows (`874c771`). I use two
  same-session runs of `main` instead: ⁸⁸ is several merges old (#403's parts 2–3, #406), and
  CLAUDE.md's own caveat is that FPS ramps across back-to-back runs, so a baseline from another
  night cannot separate a 4% shader cost from the night. The 4% and the both-runs rule are the
  review's.
- **R3 — the sweep seed stays fixed in CI** (the review suggests the commit SHA). `main` is
  `strict` (CLAUDE.md, "GitHub workflow"): a PR is re-merged with `main` and re-run before it can
  land, under a new SHA each time, so a SHA seed would turn an unrelated PR red on a viewport its
  diff never touched — "CI fails for a reason you do not understand" by construction. The env
  override plus the two-other-seeds rule in §5 gives the exploration without that.
- **R4 — the far-join property is two bounds, not one equality.** The review states it as "both
  the same number of pixels at every sweep viewport, up to `ceil`'s rounding". Below ≈500 rows the
  width floor (`MIN_RING_NODES`, PR 3) holds the outer radius at 30 chunks, so the voxel cell at
  the join is *smaller* than 8.06 px there. PR 6 step 5 therefore asserts the upper bound
  everywhere and the invariance only where the floor is inactive.
- **R5 — "`Renderer::render` loops over `self.views`" is limited to views of one size.**
  `mesh.wgsl` takes `viewport_width`, `viewport_height` and `aspect` as pipeline `override`s
  (`mesh.wgsl:66-68`), and `SceneRenderer::resize` rebuilds the pipeline and the depth buffer
  synchronously for a new size (`scene.rs:466-481`), for the M3 reason recorded at
  `render.rs:1453-1462`. One renderer therefore draws N views of its target's viewport that differ
  in direction (PR 4 step 6); views of differing sizes need a pipeline and depth buffer per size,
  which is what "one per monitor" in a real multi-window setup needs and what the issue defers
  (*"Opening several real windows or monitors at once is the windowing layer's job"*). The culling
  and LOD properties are proven for any per-view FOV at the GPU-free level (PR 4 step 9).
